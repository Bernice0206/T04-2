Television Energy Tips

Screen size and technology can affect how much energy a television uses. Understanding these factors can help consumers make better choices when purchasing a TV.

How to Reduce TV Energy Use

Use Energy Saving Mode

Use the television's Energy Saver or Eco mode when available to reduce power consumption.

Adjust Brightness

Reducing screen brightness can help lower the amount of energy used by the television.

Check Energy Ratings

Energy ratings and annual energy consumption information can help consumers compare different television models.

TV Energy Insights: Data Story

How do television models in Australia differ in terms of technology, size, brand and energy consumption?

This data story uses the cleaned Australian television dataset to explore six questions that may be useful to Australian TV consumers. The visualisations help show which TV features are most common and how screen size, technology and star rating relate to power consumption.

The cleaned dataset contains 4,759 available television models.

1. What screen technologies are currently available in Australia?

Key finding:

LCD (LED) was the most frequently available screen technology, with 3,794 models. LCD had 496 models, while OLED had 292 models.

2. What screen sizes are currently available, and which is the most frequent?

Key finding:

The most frequent TV screen size was 65 inches, with 777 television models.

3. Which brands have the greatest number of different models?

Key finding:

KOGAN had the greatest number of different TV models, with 807 models, followed by LG with 723 models.

4. Which screen technology has the lowest median power consumption?

Key finding:

LCD televisions had the lowest median power consumption at 71.3472 W. LCD (LED) had a median power consumption of 106 W, while OLED had 112.7 W.

5. What is the relationship between screen size and power use?

Key finding:

The scatter plot shows a general positive relationship between screen size and power consumption. Larger televisions generally tend to use more power, although there is variation between individual models.

6. What is the relationship between star rating and screen size?

Key finding:

The points are spread across different screen sizes, showing no clear consistent relationship between screen size and star rating in this dataset.

Conclusion

The six visualisations show that television models can vary in technology, size, brand and power consumption. LCD (LED) was the most frequently available screen technology, while 65 inches was the most common screen size. KOGAN had the greatest number of different models in the dataset.

LCD had the lowest median power consumption among the screen technologies. The data also showed a general positive relationship between screen size and power consumption, while there was no clear relationship between screen size and star rating.
